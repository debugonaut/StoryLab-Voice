Abstract—This document presents the complete Software
Requirements Specification (SRS) forStoryLab V oice(Release
1.0), structured in strict alignment with the standard IEEE
Std 830-1998 SRS template and ISO/IEC 25010:2011 quality
guidelines. StoryLab Voice is an offline-first, voice-guided educa-
tional platform developed for resource-constrained rural schools
where physical laboratory facilities and reliable broadband
connectivity are unavailable. Centered around an empathetic
AI Voice Assistant Chatbot, the platform drives two primary
pedagogical modules:StoryLearn(an interactive, multi-subject
storytelling engine across Math, Science, Social Studies, and EVS)
andLabInBox(a 2D voice-guided virtual science experiment
simulator). This specification formally delineates product scope,
external interface requirements, detailed system features with
priority ratings, and non-functional constraints. Furthermore, it
provides a comprehensive process model identification, selecting
and analyzing the Spiral Model as the optimal risk-driven
development methodology.
Index Terms—Software Requirements Specification (SRS),
IEEE Std 830-1998, Conversational AI, Voice-Guided Learning,

D. Product Scope
StoryLab V oice is an offline-first educational platform en-
gineered for rural schools. Rather than requiring students to
navigate complex graphical menus or parse dense textual
material, students interact directly with an empathetic AI
V oice Chatbot Companion. The chatbot serves as an always-
available personal tutor: narrating localized educational stories,
posing guiding questions, answering spontaneous queries in
real time, and guiding students step-by-step through virtual
science experiments.
Primary Objectives & Goals:
1) Provide a conversational, judgment-free voice interface
serving as an always-available personal tutor for rural
students.
2) Eliminate physical laboratory equipment dependencies by
offering interactive virtual simulations directed entirely via
voice dialogue.
3) Contextualize multi-subject syllabus curricula into local-
ized audio stories orchestrated by the chatbot engine.

II. OVERALLDESCRIPTION
A. Product Perspective
StoryLab V oice is architected around a central Conversa-
tional AI Chatbot layer. The chatbot serves as the primary
user interface, coordinating real-time bidirectional data flow
between local offline speech models, the LabInBox simulation
canvas, and the StoryLearn curriculum database.
B. Product Functions
The principal functional capabilities provided by StoryLab
V oice include:
•Continuous Voice Interaction:Real-time speech recogni-
tion (STT) and local voice synthesis (TTS) for hands-free
pedagogical engagement.
•Contextual Academic Tutoring:Answering curriculum
queries dynamically in simplified vernacular or English.
•Interactive Story-Based Learning:Branching educational
narrative delivery across four academic subjects (Math,
Science, Social Studies, EVS).

C. User Classes and Characteristics
•Rural Students (Ages 6–15):Primary end-users who inter-
act by speaking to and listening to the chatbot companion
in their native vernacular language or simplified English.
•Rural Teachers / NGO Facilitators:Supervisors who
monitor conversation summaries, assign syllabus mod-
ules/experiments, and track learning analytics through a
dedicated dashboard.
•System Administrators:Technical staff who manage re-
gional voice synthesis models, maintain curriculum map-
pings, and audit AI response accuracy.
D. Operating Environment
•Client Hardware:Android 8.0+ smartphones and tablets
equipped with a minimum of 2 GB RAM, integrated micro-
phone, and audio speaker hardware.
•Supported Browsers:Chromium-based browsers (Google
Chrome 88+, Microsoft Edge), and Progressive Web App
(PW A) runtime containers.
•Network Constraints:Full functionality in disconnected
offline settings using lightweight on-device speech synthe-
sis, accompanied by periodic analytics synchronization over
intermittent 2G/3G cellular links.
E. Design and Implementation Constraints
•Storage Limits:Total local footprint (PW A assets, audio
caches, and lightweight acoustic models) must strictly re-

III. EXTERNALINTERFACEREQUIREMENTS
A. User Interfaces
•Conversational Voice Interface:Primary bidirectional
voice interaction loop enabling 100% hands-free navigation.
•Visual Avatar / Waveform:Animated visual feedback
indicating real-time chatbot states:Listening,Thinking, and
Speaking.
•Lab Canvas UI:Clean 2D graphical apparatus display
responsive to touch and voice triggers.
B. Hardware Interfaces
•Standard3.5mm audio jack / USB-C / built-in microphone
for voice capture (16kHz mono sampling).
•Built-in loudspeaker or external earphones for TTS audio
playback.
C. Software Interfaces
•Local Storage API:HTML5IndexedDBfor storing les-
son state, student progress, and local voice response caches.
•Audio APIs:W3C Web Audio API and WebRTC for low-
latency audio capture and waveform visualization.
•Service Worker Engine:CacheStorage API for offline
PW A asset caching.
D. Communications Interfaces
•Sync Protocol:RESTful HTTPS with compressed JSON
payloads.
•Network Resilience:Background Sync API handling inter-
mittent 2G/3G connectivity without blocking UI.

VI. PROCESSMODELIDENTIFICATION& ANALYSIS
A. Introduction
StoryLab V oice is an offline-first educational platform de-
signed specifically for rural schools. It combines an AI V oice
Assistant Chatbot, StoryLearn for interactive multi-subject
stories, and LabInBox for voice-guided virtual science exper-
iments.
The engineering of this platform encompasses several tech-
nically challenging and interdependent components:
•On-device Speech-to-Text (STT) and Text-to-Speech (TTS)
processing under tight hardware limits.
•Conversational AI dialogue management and contextual
state tracking.
•Robust offline functionality with asynchronous, bidirectional
cloud data synchronization.
•Real-time voice-controlled 2D virtual physics and chemistry
experiment simulations.
•Dynamic and adaptive pedagogical learning algorithms tai-
lored to varied student comprehension levels.
Consequently, the chosen software development life cycle
(SDLC) process model must facilitate incremental delivery
while systematically identifying, evaluating, and mitigating
technical risks.
B. Identified Process Model: The Spiral Model
Identified Process Model: Spiral Model(Boehm, 1988).
TheSpiral Modelis identified as the most suitable software
process model for StoryLab V oice. It uniquely merges the
iterative nature of rapid prototyping with the controlled, sys-
tematic aspects of linear development models, while anchor-
ing every iteration aroundexplicit risk analysis. The system
evolves through concentric spiral cycles, where each loop
progressively elaborates requirements, analyzes risks, builds
prototypes, and validates deliverables with end users.

C. Justification and Risk Analysis
The adoption of the Spiral Model for StoryLab V oice is
justified across six foundational engineering dimensions:
1)High Technical Risk Management:StoryLab V oice in-
tegrates complex edge AI voice interaction, offline speech

inference, dynamic conversational logic, and real-time vir-
tual laboratory simulations on low-cost hardware. The
Spiral Model ensures that high-risk technical bottlenecks
(such as audio latency and memory overflows) are isolated,
analyzed, and mitigated in early spiral loops prior to full-
scale engineering.
2)Prototyping & Incremental Feasibility:Rapid prototyp-
ing is embedded into each cycle. A standalone functional
prototype of the AI V oice Assistant Chatbot Core can be
built, evaluated on low-end target hardware, and bench-
marked before committing development resources to the
complex virtual laboratory canvas.
3)Structured Incremental Development Phases:The sys-
tem is engineered logically across five sequential spiral
iterations:
Phase 1:AI Voice Assistant Chatbot Core:Build
STT/TTS pipelines, voice activity detection,
and conversational state tracking.
Phase 2:StoryLearn Multi-Subject Story Engine:Im-
plement branching narratives, dynamic audio
streaming, and localized cultural variables.
Phase 3:LabInBox Virtual Science Simulator:Develop
HTML5/Canvas 2D simulation physics and map
spoken intents to canvas interactions.
Phase 4:Offline Storage, Local Caching & Cloud Syn-
chronization:ImplementIndexedDBcaching,
conflict resolution, and background syncing over
2G/3G networks.
Phase 5:End-to-End System Integration & Field Testing:
Execute full-system integration, regression vali-
dation, performance profiling, and usability trials
in rural classrooms.
4)Continuous User Feedback Loops:Rural students and ed-
ucators evaluate each spiral release. This regular feedback
loop allows developers to iteratively calibrate speech recog-
nition accuracy for regional accents, refine pedagogical
dialogue tones, and enhance accessibility for non-technical
users.
5)Adaptability to Changing Requirements:Elements such
as regional language dialects, state syllabus curriculum
revisions, and ambient classroom noise profiles will in-
evitably evolve during field trials. The Spiral Model ef-
fortlessly accommodates modifications at each planning
boundary.
6)Early Testing & Multi-Stage Validation:Every spiral
iteration culminates in rigorous formal verification. Perfor-
mance defects—such as latency spikes (>1.8s), speech
recognition mismatches, and animation rendering lags—are
caught and resolved early in the lifecycle rather than during
final deployment.
